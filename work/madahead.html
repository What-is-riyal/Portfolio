Product brief

Give friends a structured reason to create something together.

MadAhead was based on the pass-the-story game, where each person adds one line before passing the story to the next player. The brief was to define the audience and positioning, then design the complete digital experience.

Players would join a room, respond to a prompt, and take turns adding lines within a time limit. Reactions, titles, and publishing extended the experience after the story was complete.

Competitive analysis

MadAhead combined social play with structured creative writing.

I reviewed products offering collaborative writing, creative play, and social storytelling. The comparison helped position MadAhead as a time-limited social game rather than an open-ended writing platform.

Concept evaluation

I tested the game before designing the product.

01

WhatsApp concept evaluation

Purpose
Understand whether people would participate in a collaborative story game and how different prompts affected play.
Method
I evaluated the concept over WhatsApp with 24 participants aged 19 to 26.
Output
I documented completion time, participation, and responses to different types of story prompts.

Audience research

Three needs helped define the product benefits.

I combined the concept evaluation with five unstructured interviews. Instead of treating participants as fixed personas, I organized the product strategy around the needs the game could serve.

01

Social connection

A shared activity that gave friends something to do together beyond conversation.

02

Writing practice

A short, structured format for people who wanted to write without beginning a large project.

03

Creative prompts

A low-pressure way to generate ideas and work through creative blocks.

Feature prioritization

The initial release focused on completing and sharing a story.

We used the MoSCoW framework to prioritize features based on the concept evaluation, competitive analysis, development time, and technical feasibility.

Must

Rooms, prompts, turn-taking, reactions, and story completion.

Later

Community progression

Publishing, discovery, leaderboards, XP, and additional game modes.

Interaction design

The interface supported the complete game loop.

After defining the audience and feature priorities, I developed the information architecture, wireframes, visual design, and supporting states for the game.

Usability testing

Six participants completed four tasks in the product prototype.

The study evaluated whether participants could move through the primary game and community flows. The overall task-success rate was 76.8%.

Product outcome

The product launched, evolved through several pivots, and was eventually discontinued.

MadAhead received a few hundred downloads. The team continued iterating after this phase and later shifted the product from gaming toward education.

Product lesson

A social entertainment product needs a clear distribution strategy and enough active participation to sustain the experience.

What I learned

Designing complete flows for implementation

This was my first extended experience preparing interface designs for developer handoff. Early gaps in edge cases and supporting screens taught me to document complete flows and states before implementation.
