Razorpay is a 7.5 Billion dollars Indian start up solving for 8M+ businesses' end to end money movements

Project Background

Project Background

• With the onset of Covid when online businesses boomed, many small businesses wanted to ride the wave and go digital. These novice internet users were struggling to complete the rigorous onboarding forms of Razorpay which involved collecting many business details for KYC. This untapped segment could give a big jump to the customer base of the company.

• The objective was to increase our monthly transacting users and decrease the support tickets raised during onboarding

Understanding the problem

Understanding the problem

To understand the problem better,
• I observed over 50 merchants filling the onboarding forms through Hotjar
• I dissected 150+ support tickets of merchants and created an affinity map to identify the main problem buckets.
• Conducted interviews with the support team to understand the qualitative anecdotes, and held an empathy workshop with cross functional peers to create a list of hypotheses
• Did a competitive analysis to understand the state of art of the KYC onboarding forms, their fields and phrasing.

Competitive analysis format · Empathy workshop on FigJam

Learnings

Learnings

  • • The number of questions in the form are overwhelming
  • • Many fields are not commonly understood in this user group and often need clarification
  • • Users are hesitant to share information without knowing how it would be used.
  • • Few flows were not optimised for mobile leading to a bad experience.

Methodology

Methodology

1. Analysed support tickets to create buckets of problems and metrics to dientify drop off points
2. Conducted teardown workshop for granular friction points
3. Conducted role play to learn from offline interaction
4. Established fundamental design principles to follow brainstorming

1. Analysing support tickets

Analysed 150+ support tickets to identify main problem buckets and the why people get stuck.
The current onboarding forms were long, complex, and jargon-filled. Merchants felt overwhelmed by the experience and were seeking human assistance.

2. Workshop

To identify a granular level of hurdles, I conducted an empathy workshop with product, design and writing teams where everyone wore the hat of a merchant and identified friction points. We arrived at a priority list of problems through dot voting and support ticket analysis.

3. Competitor Analysis

To understand the state of art of the KYC onboarding forms, we conducted a competitor analysis and studied the features, fields they require and how they are phrased.

What did we learn?

  • Overwhelmed by the number of questions on the onboarding screen and sought human assistance.
  • Struggled to understand the one-word fields without clarification.
  • Hesitant to share information without knowing how it would be used.
  • Unsure of their progress through the onboarding process.

3. Role playing

To replicate how people fill forms offline with human assistance, we conducted a role play exercise of opening a bank account and analysed crucial points like the tone, the approach and manner of handholding.

Final Principles

Final Principles

As we started brainstorming, we realised that to build trust and comfort while filling lengthy forms, we needed to design an experience that out user cohort was familiar with.
We conducted a role play activity among us to document how a Bank account is opened with a relationship manager. This formed the basis of our design principles.

Mobile first

Since mobile is the primary device for this cohort, optimise the entire form for this breakpoint. Previously our platform was optimised primarily for desktop.

Replicate human interaction

Display each field on a separate screen and framed as a question

Progressive Disclosure

Follow progressive disclosure for each question to reduce the cognitive load

Build Trust

Add cautionary notes for sensitive questions to explain why the information was needed and how it would be used.

Usability test

Usability test

Since the new designs were a huge shift from the existing experience, I pushed to conduct a usability test with a fresh set of merchants. The test was broken into 3 parts:
• Evaluate usability of the easy onboarding
• Qualitative probing to understand the mental model, perceptions and behavior of merchants while signing up
• Validate the conversational onboarding format through a preference test

We spoke to 11 merchants across industries and geographies. 73% participants preferred the new onboarding and we got granular insights on what could be improved further.

Learnings

Learnings

Given the chance, there are a few things I would change about how we did this. Due to the strict timelines, with the first primary research conducted directly in the end, it became harder to incorporate the insights in time. Better planning could have made this more efficient.

This project helped me sharpen my quantitative skills and gave me the opportunity to innovate. This was one of the most interesting problems where I was forced to look at how information is accessed, consumed and processed by people in different contexts.
