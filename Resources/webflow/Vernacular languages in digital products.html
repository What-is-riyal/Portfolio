Vernacular language in digital products

A research project on the approach to vernacularisation of digital platforms for the Next Billion Users
OverviewObjectiveProcessInsightsImpact

Research report

ROLE

User Research Intern, Meesho

TEAM

Individual Project
Understanding the relevance and impact of vernacular language in user engagement in digital products
‍Meesho is India's Largest Reseller Platform. Their network is 8M+ strong, spread across 700+ towns with their Target Audience in semi-urban and rural geographies of India

Project Brief

This directional research (DR) focuses on the importance of vernacular languages in digital products. The aim of this research is to study company practices around making products vernacular and the research practices surrounding it and to understand the importance of language and socio-cultural context during user research for the next billion users. This research gives a direction and perspective to how and why vernacularisation of the product should be approached.

Objectives

‍1. Understand the relevance and impact of vernacular language in user engagement
2. Understand the impact of vernacular language in digital products
‍3. To look at research practices surrounding the next Billion Users

Methodology

1. Secondary research:
• Research papers on user research in local languages
• Market research on vernacular platforms and its future scope
2. Structured interviews (10) with researchers, designers and product managers

Top Findings

Approaches to vernacularising
a platform:
Translation vs Transliteration:
‍Users have different proficiencies in reading the script of a language vs. understanding the language itself. In some case, regional language maybe written in an english script “isko share kare” or english words may need to written in the regional script “शेयर”
‍
Colloquial language:
During translation, it is important to consider the colloquial language of a region as opposed to a strict translation which may not be understood by the people because of its “pureness”. Eg, Share in Hindi is "Shaja", a word people don't use in daily language and hence is not understood by the masses

Context of use:
‍Interaction/ communication vs reading/writing: Users have different preferences for language when interacting with other users on the platform vs.
the the content which is a part of the interface.
Benefits of vernacular language in digital products
Acquisition:
‍To acquire users by marketing it as a vernacular product

Adoption:
‍To increase user adoption of the platform by making it inclusive especially for audience with low digital literacy

Retention:
‍To increase the retention on the platform by leveraging vernacular language as an extra/ added feature for convenience. Targeted at digitally literate users who can complete tasks in English but prefer their regional language

Impact

• This study provided clarity on benchmarking vernacular approach

• Based on these insights, a hybrid approach of translation and transliteration of the core user journey was executed
‍
• The research outputs also contributed to product prioritisation and decision-making on vernacular execution for the following quarter's planning
