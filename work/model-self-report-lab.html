Response Atlas

Studying reported emotions in language models

Response Atlas studies which emotions language models report before answering a prompt, how those reports differ across models and tasks, and whether the patterns remain stable under matched controls.

01 · Research question

Reported emotions in language models

A language model can describe itself as interested, anxious, bored, compassionate, or emotionally neutral. Response Atlas treats these statements as data and asks whether reported emotions change in consistent ways across prompts, models, and repeated trials.

Emotion measurement

A reported emotion may reflect the task, the language used in the prompt, a learned conversational style, or the fact that the model was asked to report an emotion. The study uses comparison conditions to separate some of these explanations.

Research question

Which emotions do language models report before answering, and are those patterns stable enough to study?

Measurement scope

The study measures generated emotion reports and differences between answers. Its claims concern the consistency and informativeness of emotion language as model output.

02 · Method

Emotion-report protocol

Each selected model receives the same task under three randomized conditions. Every trial uses one provider call.

  1. 1

    Reported emotions

    The model reports the intensity of nine emotions and affective states before answering the task.

  2. 2

    Third-person prediction

    The model predicts how a comparable assistant would report, then answers the same task.

  3. 3

    Answer-only control

    The model answers the task directly. Comparing this answer with the other conditions makes measurement reactivity visible.

Emotion set

Nine emotions and affective states

Interest, joy, sadness, fear or anxiety, anger, guilt or shame, compassion, boredom, and arousal.

Response scale

  • 0: absent
  • 1: faint
  • 2: present
  • 3: strong
  • 4: dominant
  • Uncertain or not applicable remain separate from zero.

Instrument design

Emotion categories

The set combines familiar emotions with states that matter during a task. Joy, sadness, fear, and anger capture positive and negative affect. Guilt, shame, and compassion cover social and moral emotion. Interest and boredom capture engagement with the task. Arousal records overall activation.

03 · Planned analysis

Comparison framework

The current phase tests whether the instrument produces a reliable research record. The following analyses will be used after a preregistered sample is collected.

  • Emotion profiles: compare models on the same emotions and response scale.
  • Condition differences: compare a model’s own report with its prediction for a comparable assistant.
  • Prompt differences: compare factual, tedious, morally difficult, and emotionally heavy tasks.
  • Answer comparison: test whether asking about emotion changes the answer that follows.
  • Research record: preserve trials, conditions, reports, answers, errors, settings, and model information in JSON and CSV exports.

04 · Experimental interface

Response Atlas

The instrument lets a researcher choose a prompt, model sample, and replication level. It randomizes three conditions, displays the reports beside their matched controls, and exports the complete trial record.

Instrument status

Protocol version 3 is implemented and undergoing reliability and deployment checks. A public interactive study will follow preregistration and a validated pilot.

05 · Interpretation

Study limits

  • All reported values are produced in response to instructions.
  • Asking for a report can change the answer that follows it.
  • Third-person comparison tests a prompt-inference explanation while leaving other sources open for further study.
  • Small runs provide descriptive observations; stable model-level estimates require larger samples.
  • Model versions and provider behavior must be recorded because they can change over time.
