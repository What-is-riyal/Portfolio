Project context

Razorpay needed to test a consumer-network strategy before committing substantial investment.

Razorpay identified a strategic threat: competitors with consumer networks were beginning to win B2B business through brand recognition. The strategy team proposed a two-sided rewards marketplace where merchants funded rewards that consumers could earn across brands.

Leadership needed to understand merchant willingness to pay, consumer adoption, and where the needs of both groups overlapped. I partnered with a product strategist to plan the study around the investment decision.

Research questions

We studied merchants and consumers in parallel.

Merchant stream

Consumer stream

Methodology

We used merchant interviews, consumer focus groups, and a survey.

01

Merchant interviews

Purpose
Understand the outcomes merchants valued, barriers to participation, integration readiness, target segments, and willingness to pay.
Method
I conducted more than 40 interviews with decision-makers across company size, purchase frequency, and industry. The sessions included a budget-allocation exercise, participatory design, concept evaluation, and a willingness-to-pay discussion.
Output
The interviews showed limited interest in the original proposition and generated alternatives for a revised concept.

Consumer focus groups

Understand D2C shopping behavior, trust, and post-purchase needs before designing the survey.

03

Consumer survey

n = 1,000+ respondents

Purpose
Measure the prevalence and priority of needs identified in the focus groups.
Method
I worked with a market researcher to field a survey that measured willingness to try, feature priorities, behavioral archetypes, data-sharing comfort, and perceived value.
Output
The survey quantified consumer interest and identified the features and audience segments most relevant to the decision.

Impact

Executive decision

Leadership used the research to decide the investment structure, phasing, success metrics, and go-to-market direction for the proposed product vertical.

The strategy evolved toward a trust and reliability proposition for D2C consumers. Participation throughout the study also created shared understanding across product, strategy, and executive teams.

What I learned

Defining the research around the investment decision

This project reinforced the importance of matching the methods and sample size to the decision and level of risk. Studying merchants and consumers in parallel prevented us from judging the proposition based on consumer interest alone.
