Project context

Razorpay needed to test a consumer-network strategy before committing substantial investment.

Razorpay identified a strategic threat: competitors with consumer networks were beginning to win B2B business through brand recognition. The strategy team proposed a two-sided rewards marketplace where merchants funded rewards that consumers could earn across brands.

Leadership required evidence about merchant willingness to pay, consumer adoption, and the point where those interests could converge. I partnered with a product strategist to define what evidence would be sufficient for the investment decision.

Research architecture

Two research streams tested a single marketplace proposition from opposite sides.

Merchant stream

Would merchants fund it?

Identify valuable outcomes, barriers to participation, integration readiness, target segments, and willingness to pay.

Shared value
Consumer stream

Would consumers adopt it?

Measure willingness to try, comfort with data sharing, trust drivers, shopping pain points, and feature priorities.

Qualitative exploration established the model; quantitative research tested its scale.

The pivotal finding

The original rewards proposition generated little merchant interest.

This was not a failed study outcome. The participatory design work identified a different model that could create value for merchants, consumers, and Razorpay. The decision shifted from validating the proposed feature to reframing the strategic opportunity.

Decision outputs

The synthesis turned two evidence streams into a focused investment thesis.

  • 01

    Defined the consumers most likely to adopt.

    Behavioral archetypes clarified where trust and post-purchase pain points created meaningful demand.

  • 02

    Separated must-have features from later opportunities.

    Feature priorities provided guidance for MVP scope and sequencing.

  • 03

    Reframed what merchants would pay for.

    The research replaced the original assumption with a value proposition grounded in merchant outcomes.

  • 04

    Narrowed the viable merchant segments.

    The go-to-market strategy could focus on contexts with the strongest likelihood of success.

Impact

The research informed the executive decision to proceed—and changed what proceeding meant.

  • aLeadership used the findings to make decisions about investment structure, phasing, and success metrics rather than a binary go/no-go choice.
  • bThe strategy evolved from a generic rewards platform toward a trust and reliability proposition for D2C consumers.
  • cMerchant segmentation and consumer adoption patterns directly informed a more targeted go-to-market strategy.
  • dStakeholder participation throughout research and synthesis created shared understanding across product, strategy, and executive teams.

What I learned

Research quality depends on defining what evidence is sufficient for the decision.

This project reinforced that strategic research should be proportional to the risk being tested and should resist premature validation. Working across consumers and merchants also showed how easily a two-sided proposition can appear desirable when one side's incentives are left implicit.
