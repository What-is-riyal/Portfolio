Project context

Razorpay redesigned its homepage to reach smaller businesses coming online for the first time.

Razorpay was known primarily for its payment gateway and had expanded into neobanking, payroll, and lending. The redesign aimed to communicate this broader product offer and make the company more relatable to smaller, less technically experienced businesses.

I proposed an evaluative study to test brand perception, product comprehension, and usability before launch.

Research questions

The study addressed three research questions.

  • 01

    Did the homepage communicate the intended brand position?

    We evaluated brand perception separately from visual preference.

  • 02

    Did the visuals and content feel relatable and understandable?

    We tested what the visual language communicated before participants saw the final copy.

  • 03

    Could people find and understand the expanded product offer?

    We assessed product discovery, comprehension, and task completion.

01

Design pre-mortem

Purpose
Identify assumptions and likely failure points before testing the prototype with participants.
Method
I facilitated a design pre-mortem with design, content, and product stakeholders.
Output
The workshop produced a set of knowledge gaps and testable hypotheses for the study.

02

UX audit

Purpose
Resolve usability issues that did not require participant research.
Method
I conducted a UX audit with product designers from across the organization.
Output
We separated immediate fixes from hypotheses that needed to be tested with participants.

03

User interviews and prototype testing

Purpose
Evaluate brand perception, product comprehension, and usability as separate research questions.
Method
I conducted remote, blinded interviews with ten business owners who were not using Razorpay. Each 50-minute session included four exercises.
Output
The study identified issues with first impressions, visual communication, product discovery, and the overall experience.

Findings and recommendations

The study identified changes needed across content, interaction design, and brand.

The detailed findings remain under NDA. The recommendations addressed how clearly the homepage communicated Razorpay's expanded product offer and how content and interaction design supported that understanding.

01

Interaction predictability

Keep scrolling and navigation behavior predictable.

Participants were confused when vertical scrolling triggered horizontal movement. We recommended simpler interactions that behaved as expected.

02

Product comprehension

Explain how the products fit together.

Participants struggled to understand the expanded portfolio and differences between products. We recommended clearer terminology and content hierarchy.

03

Product discovery

Make key products and actions easier to find.

Task-based testing identified products and calls to action that participants overlooked. We recommended changes to navigation and placement.

Impact

After launch, website conversion increased and most recommendations were implemented.

What I learned

Working with stakeholders throughout the study

Involving cross-functional partners in the design pre-mortem, research sessions, and synthesis helped the team understand the findings and clarified ownership of the recommended changes.
