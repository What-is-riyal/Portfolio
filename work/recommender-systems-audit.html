CORDA

A common testbed for alternative feed rankers

Academic researchers have proposed many alternatives to engagement-based ranking. CORDA brings these approaches into one catalog and evaluates them in the same simulated environment using shared measures.

01 · Research problem

Fragmented evidence on alternative rankers

Academic work already contains chronological, bridging, diversity-oriented, toxicity-aware, and other ranking methods. These proposals are spread across papers and are often evaluated with different datasets and metrics. Policy teams must first find the alternatives, understand how each one works, and determine whether the reported results can be compared.

Policy need

A shared repository can make alternative rankers easier to discover. A common testbed can then compare their effects under the same conditions, giving policymakers a clearer view of benefits, risks, and tradeoffs.

Research question

How can alternative feed-ranking algorithms be documented and compared using one set of policy-relevant measures?

02 · Research infrastructure

Catalog and common testbed

CORDA combines two forms of infrastructure: a structured record of alternative ranking methods and a simulation for comparing selected methods under consistent conditions.

  1. 1

    Ranker catalog

    For each method, record its objective, required inputs, scoring rule, source paper, expected benefit, known tradeoffs, and evaluation evidence.

  2. 2

    Common testbed

    Implement selected methods in the same environment and compare them using shared populations, seed content, interaction rules, and outcome measures.

Initial experiment

Controlled social-media simulation

Six Mastodon servers receive the same agent population, interaction rules, and COVID-19 seed corpus. Each server uses a different ranking rule.

Agents
3,000 personas based on European Social Survey and Eurostat data
Seed content
Approximately 1,300 public posts about COVID-19
Agent actions
Post, reply, favorite, boost, or quote
Feed
20 posts selected from the 250 most recent posts

Experimental control

The agent population, starting content, and interaction budget remain the same across conditions. Differences between the six simulations can therefore be compared with less interference from sampling or exposure.

03 · Initial catalog

Six ranking rules

The first comparison includes a neutral time-based baseline, a conventional engagement ranker, three proposed interventions, and an intentionally polarizing condition.

  1. 1

    Chronological

    Shows the newest posts first.

  2. 2

    Engagement

    Ranks replies, quotes, favorites, and boosts, with more weight on resharing.

  3. 3

    Bridging

    Raises posts that receive approval from users with different interaction histories.

  4. 4

    Homophily

    Raises posts favored by users similar to the viewer, creating an intentional echo-chamber condition.

  5. 5

    Lower outrage

    Reduces the rank of posts that combine moral language with anger or disgust.

  6. 6

    Lower toxicity

    Reduces the rank of posts with a higher toxicity score.

04 · Evaluation framework

Shared measures

The measures cover both public-interest outcomes and implementation constraints. Using the same measures for each ranker makes tradeoffs visible.

Engagement
Policy feasibility. Measures favorites, replies, views, boosts, and quotes. A ranker that improves public-interest measures but sharply reduces participation may face resistance from platforms, so engagement must be evaluated rather than assumed.
Conversation size
Depth of interaction. Measures the number and structure of replies alongside lightweight reactions.
Cross-group approval
Bridging. Tests whether a post receives positive engagement from users with different interaction histories.
Moral outrage
Emotional amplification. Tracks moral language combined with anger or disgust because this combination is associated with online diffusion.
Toxicity and hate speech
Discourse quality. Tests whether a ranking rule changes the circulation of hostile or abusive content.
Conspiracy content
Information quality. Measures the spread of conspiracy narratives in the COVID-19 seed corpus.

Engagement as an implementation constraint

Engagement is both an outcome and an implementation constraint. Measuring it shows whether an alternative ranker can improve other outcomes while maintaining enough participation to be practical for platforms and acceptable to users.

05 · Preliminary results

Feed-level comparison

The current evidence comes from one 1,123-tick exploratory run. It demonstrates the comparison workflow and surfaces several results that the longer, repeated experiment must investigate.

Implementation result
Six ranking rules ran against the same population, seed corpus, candidate window, and interaction rules. The experiment can therefore expose directional differences under controlled conditions.
Unexpected result
The chronological condition produced the highest rolling bridging score. This result raises a calibration or construct-validity question for the bridging intervention.
Adoption constraint
The engagement distributions overlap across conditions in this run. Repeated runs are needed to estimate whether public-interest interventions preserve participation reliably.
Inference boundary
These plots describe one simulated run. The next analysis will report repeated-run uncertainty before drawing conclusions about ranker performance.

07 · Policy relevance

Digital Services Act

The European Union’s Digital Services Act creates a policy setting in which comparative evidence about recommendation systems can be useful.

  • Recommender transparency: Article 27 requires platforms to explain the main parameters used in their recommender systems and the options users have to influence them.
  • Systemic-risk assessment: very large platforms must assess risks connected to the design and use of their services, including recommendation systems.
  • Risk mitigation: platforms must adopt reasonable and proportionate measures for the risks they identify.
  • Alternative option: Article 38 requires very large platforms using recommender systems to offer at least one option that is not based on profiling.
  • Research access: Article 40 and the 2025 delegated act provide a route for vetted researchers to request platform data for research on systemic risks.

Potential use

A catalog and common testbed could help researchers identify candidate mitigation measures, compare their tradeoffs, and prepare evidence for a platform risk assessment or vetted-researcher data request.

08 · Interpretation

Study limits

  • LLM agents provide controlled behavioral proxies for this comparison.
  • The reported results cover 1,123 ticks; the planned experiment covers 5,000.
  • The measures are proxies for properties such as bridging, outrage, toxicity, and conspiracy content.
  • Personas use 2023 survey data while the simulated discussion is set in 2020.
  • A human-participant study will test which patterns carry into real use.
