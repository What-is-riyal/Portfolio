Project context

One million daily visitors would encounter a broader Razorpay through a new homepage.

Razorpay was known primarily for its payment gateway, but the company had expanded into neobanking, payroll, and lending. The homepage redesign aimed to shift that perception and become more relatable to smaller, less technically experienced businesses.

I proposed a comprehensive evaluation because a change of this scale required evidence about comprehension and usability—not only internal confidence in the design direction.

Research questions

The study separated three questions that the redesign had bundled together.

  • 01

    Did the homepage communicate the intended brand position?

    Test the direction without assuming visual preference meant strategic comprehension.

  • 02

    Did the visuals and content feel relatable and understandable?

    Isolate what the visual language communicated before adding meaningful copy.

  • 03

    Could people find and understand the expanded product offer?

    Identify usability issues and test specific product-discovery hypotheses.

01

Design pre-mortem

Why
Surface assumptions and likely failure points before spending participant time on the prototype.
What
A workshop with design, content, and product stakeholders.
How
Partners imagined the redesign had failed and converted the reasons into knowledge gaps and testable hypotheses.

02

UX audit

Why
Remove obvious issues so sessions could focus on questions that required user evidence.
What
An internal review with product designers from across the organisation.
How
Reviewers separated immediate hygiene fixes from hypotheses for primary research.

03

Primary research

Why
Evaluate perception, comprehension, and usability without conflating those distinct questions.
What
Remote, blinded interviews with ten business owners who were not using Razorpay.
How
Each 50-minute session moved through four deliberately sequenced tests, from first impression to direct comparison.

  • 03.1

    Impression test

    A stripped-down first fold appeared for ten seconds, followed by unaided recall to identify what attracted attention.

  • 03.2

    Visual perception test

    A static site with placeholder copy isolated what the visual language communicated about the company, audience, and brand.

  • 03.3

    Usability test

    Free exploration and guided tasks tested comprehension, product discovery, brand ratings, descriptors, and the SUS.

  • 03.4

    Preference test

    A comparison with the previous website measured perceived improvement and the reasons behind each participant's preference.

04

Synthesis and action

Why
Keep findings from ending as a generic list of usability issues.
What
A report organised around decisions for content, product, brand, and design owners.
How
I connected evidence across exercises, prioritised recommendations, and worked with partners to close the loop into the final experience.

What changed

The redesign could not succeed through visual polish alone.

The detailed findings remain under NDA. The central shift was public: I raised questions about whether people understood Razorpay's broader offer and pushed the team to change core content and interaction decisions, not only the look and feel.

Impact

The research changed both the shipped experience and how the team evaluated it.

10%increase in overall website conversion
90%of recommendations incorporated across content, design, and branding

What I learned

Stakeholder participation made the research easier to act on.

The hardest part was closing the loop between evidence and final design under tight deadlines. Involving cross-functional partners in risk framing, observation, and synthesis built confidence in the research and clarified ownership of the changes.
