• So far, Razorpay is known primarily for their payment gateway product. Over the years, the company has expanded to neobanking, payroll as well as loan services.

Objectives

‍1. To test the new brand positioning through the website
2. To test the relatability & comprehension through visuals and content

Methodology

A mix method approach was taken to ensure actionable points for each team; content, visual communication and product design. Along with pre-mortem and UX audit, I conducted multiple small tests with participants to get targeted insights.

1. Design Pre-mortem

Conducted a workshop with design, content and product stakeholders to pre-empt where the designs could fail. This helped highlight current knowledge gaps and hypotheses.

2. UX Audit

Conducted an internal design audit with product designers across the organisation to identify hygiene issues with the design and prototype.

3. Primary research

• Pariticipants: 10, business owners not using Razorpay
• Format: Remote interviews, blinded
• Duration: ~ 50 mins
• Tools: Lookback, Google Meet, Figma (prototype), Typeform

3.1 Impression test

What: Expose a stripped down version of the first fold for 10 seconds
Why: Identify the most noticeable elements of the first fold
How: Stop screenshare after 10 seconds and ask participant what all they remember seeing on the screen

3.2 Visual Perception test

What: Free exploration a static version of the website with all text replaced with Lorem Ipsum
Why: Identify the impact of visuals on brand perception and comprehension
How: Participant explores a static figma prototype. Post this,
1. Asked what they think the company does and who their audience could be.
2.  Asked to choose 5 out of 12 (combination of relevant and irrelevant) visual descriptive words that they would use to describe the visual language. [microsoft's product description cards]

3.3 Usability test

What: Free access usability followed by guided exploration
Why: Understand final brand perception, identify usability issues, test specific hypotheses
How: Participant explores the figma prototype with relevant copy and interactions
1. Asked what they think the company does and who their audience could be.
2. Given a product discovery task
3. Asked to rate the brand on 3 levers
4. Asked to choose 5 out of 12 (combination of relevant and irrelevant) brand descriptive words
5. SUS Score

3.4 Preference test

What: Comparison with previous website
Why: Identify and measure improvement
How: Participant explores the old website
1. Asked what they think the company does and who their audience could be.
2. What is different between the two website versions
3 & 4. Same as previous
5. Asked for their preference with reason

Aid for meltdowns associated with autism
A product ecosystem of a smartphone app, smartband and head/earphones to provide a variety of ways to help reduce the intensity and anxiety associated with meltdowns in people with ASD

The Problem

"A meltdown is an intense response to an overwhelming situation which may result in loss of control expressed verbally,  physically or in both ways."

During meltdowns, children with ASD often require the presence of a familiar entity like a mother. The unexpectedness of meltdowns and the child’s dependence on their guardian results in a compromised independence

The Solution

A product to condition the child to associate a custom stimuli with the feeling of familiarity and support and reduce the intensity of meltdown and dependency on guardian by auto-triggering it before the meltdown begins.

Features
What does it do?
Helps in preventing (through meltdown & cause tracking) and reducing intensity of meltdowns.
Who is it for?
Ideal to begin usage from early age(12+). Can be used by adults for self tracking & to reduce the anxiety of their loved ones.
Why do we need it?
Due to meltdowns, children with autism have limited independence and confidence since they rely heavily on their guardians and surroundings to orient themselves and calm down.
How does it work?
It works on the principle of classical conditioning. Familiar stimuli associated with relaxation gets triggered before the onset of a meltdown based on the vitals monitored by a smartband.

Research
Contextual Enquiry

To understand and identify areas of intervention, I visited a school for special children located in Indore. During a class visit, a young girl started screaming, lost control and started crying. Upon asking, I found that this was a common occurrence among many children with ASD. This was an episode of meltdown that young girl with autism went through. This presented itself as an opportunity to research deeper and find appropriate aid.
Mahila Bal Vikas Bal Graha classroom, as a part of contextual inquiry

Through the contextual enquiry, we were able to narrow down the area of work. We did a literature review of medical papers, autism specific websites, forums on social media.

We conducted 6 semi structured interviews with psychologists, teachers, parents and children with ASD to understand the methods used by them currently.

Insights

Through the contextual enquiry, literature review, and interviews we were able to gather the following key insights which gave direction to research deeper into this problem and find intervention areas.
Familiarity is sought during a meltdown for easy recovery.
Increased sensitivity to stimuli makes them location bound.
Unpredictability of meltdowns compromise independence.
They lack a healthy social life due to dependence on others.
Coping strategies and meltdown triggers vary from person to person.
Caretakers find it hard to let go and worry even as the child grows up.

Ideation

Initially we started by designing a seperate wearable for the solution but were soon struck by the limited practicality and difficulty to keep it discreet. Hence, we utilised the existing technologies and devices to come together and create a solution.

Proposed solution

The solution has three components
• Smartphone - The smart phone app ties all the components together and provides tracking.

• Smart band with a heart rate monitor, temperature and EDA sensor - The band plays the critical role of monitoring vitals and providing haptic stimulation
* LTE/wifi required for remote phone - band connection

• Headphones or earphones compatible with the smartphone - They monitor external noise and provide audio stimulation.

While all three work in harmony to provide a holistic solution, they can be used individually to leverage specific features as well. The solution covers three senses: audio, visual and haptic.
User journey
‍Rohan and his Boldband
This is Rohan. He is 15 years old and has ASD. He suffers from frequent meltdowns.
During a meltdown, his mother calms him. The unpredictability and dependency keeps him at home.
One day, his mother downloads the bold band app, connects it to the smartband and gives it to Rohan.
Through the Boldband app on her phone, she sets a vibration pattern for the band
Then, everytime Rohan has a meltdown, she alerts the app and rushes to his side as the band vibrates simultaneously
Through the reading of the  sensors in the smartband, the app learns at what levels Rohan is having a meltdown.
Over time, Rohan gets conditioned to associate the vibration pattern with his mother calming him down.
The next time he is about to have a meltdown, the stimulation starts automatically before the meltdown even begins.
If he has his earphones, a soothing voice clip/ music of his choice starts playing while slowly increasing in volume.
In case he still has a meltdown, his mother (emergency contact) is alerted.
After the meltdown, they can check the duration and input the cause of meltdown to keep a track of the triggers.
When connected to earphones, they measure the noise level continuously and alert him when  its about to get too high,
Rohan has full control of all the stimulations and can choose to turn them off anytime.
This way, Rohan and his parents felt more confident of Rohan’s ability to deal with meltdowns.
As Rohan grows up the conditioning becomes stronger and he is able to live a more independent and confident life.

Smartphone

During onboarding, the user can select their role; parent or the child. in the beginning of the product journey, parent may be the only user and has complete control which eventually reduces as the child gets his own phone and joins the ecosystem

The colour scheme was chosen based on research data available for colour preferences of people with ASD

Information Architecture

Live Status
Live status of the vitals of the child is displayed metaphorically by a boat in the sea. The calm waters denote a relaxed state, slight movement would denote a potential spike and a turbulant sea conveys immediate attention may be required.
Vitals update for medics
If required, parents can view the current vital levels by opening the boat and where they lie on the scale. They can also share them with their medical professionals.
Setting custom trigger
A combination of visual, auditory and haptic (vibration) stimulations can be set. Visual and auditory files can be uploaded or picked from the feed. For the custom vibration, a pattern can be set with varying intesity and duration.
Activity tracker
If connected with the band, after a meltdown, the app will automatically suggest the user to verify its prediction and add a cause to the meltdown if correct. The tracker makes use of emojis to give a more visual understanding to find patterns through the month.
Visual feed
The visual feed is a collection of relaxing videos which can be set as a visual trigger for the meltdowns. The video will autoplay as it senses an upcoming meltdown and be shown to the child or be played on his own app as he grows old to have his own device.
During meltdown
During meltdown child has the option to disable the auto emergency call within 10 seconds and to shut the entire stimulation in case of a false positive. During the 10 seconds, a ripple animation with be displayed for breathing control.
Noise level alert
The child can get alerts if the noise level of the room is too high and might cause him discomfort due to continuous exposure. This can be done through any mic available in earphones, smartphone or smartband.

Conclusion & learnings

Along with exploring methodologies, this project was a great opportunity to refine my stakeholder management. My biggest challenge was to close the loop between insights and final designs despite the tight deadlines we were working with.  I was able to convey the importance of UX research by involving my cross functional peers in the process throughout to build confidence.
