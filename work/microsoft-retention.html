Understanding declining usage of Outlook mobile web

The team conducted 36 interviews across six usage groups, followed by a survey with more than 300 responses. The study examined how account purpose, devices, and existing email habits affected retention.

Project context

Outlook mobile web usage was declining faster than expected.

The product was Mini OWA: the lightweight version of Outlook opened in a phone browser rather than an installed app. Consumer usage was dropping more sharply than expected. The team needed to understand what was causing the decline and the role mobile web still played for different kinds of Outlook users.

The lead researcher and product team reviewed the questions raised by stakeholders and identified what could already be answered through existing evidence. My contribution began with the interview guides and fieldwork, after this initial framing was complete.

Stakeholder hypotheses

  • Leadership, product, and engineering each had competing theories about the decline
  • The workshop identified which questions were still unanswered

Existing evidence

  • Data science reports and telemetry
  • UX audits
  • Marketing reports and prior research

Remaining questions

  • What drives loyalty and churn for each kind of user?
  • What role does mobile web play alongside the app and desktop?
  • Early read on investments already being considered: multi-account support, home-screen shortcut, notifications

Methodology

A sequential mixed-method study across six usage cohorts

The team reviewed previous research and product data, conducted interviews, and used a survey to assess the qualitative findings with a larger sample. The final synthesis informed product recommendations and planning.

01

Research framing

Purpose
Identify which questions about declining usage had already been answered and what required additional research.
Method
The team reviewed data-science reports, telemetry, UX audits, marketing reports, and previous research, then held a hypothesis workshop.
Output
The team defined the research objectives and six cohorts based on usage across Outlook mobile web and other email access points.

02

Moderated interviews

Purpose
Understand what supported retention or contributed to churn, and the role mobile web played alongside apps, desktop, and third-party clients.
Method
We conducted remote 1:1 interviews with 36 participants across six behavior-based cohorts. Cohort-specific guides covered account purpose, devices and access points, changes in use over time, current problems, and reactions to product directions under consideration.
Output
The interviews explained how account purpose, email habits, and access-point choice affected mobile web usage.

Key findings

Account type and purpose affected where and how often people checked email.

Participants often managed several email accounts with different purposes. The study grouped these accounts into four broad types. Their importance and content affected how frequently they were checked and whether people used mobile web, an app, desktop, or a third-party client.

01

Primary

The account tied to daily life and important communication.

Checked frequently
02

Secondary

An alternate address used for selected sign-ups, services, or backup.

Used for specific tasks
03

Tertiary

An older account crowded with promotions, subscriptions, and junk.

Checked when needed
04

Abandoned

A dormant account that still matters for an occasional recovery or reset.

Used rarely

Outlook mobile web served as a fallback within a broader email ecosystem.

  • Access to older accountsEspecially Hotmail accounts that had shifted from primary to secondary or tertiary use.
  • Access when apps were unavailableParticipants used mobile web when installed clients were slow, out of sync, or unavailable.
  • A lightweight optionNo installation and less storage made it useful on constrained or aging phones.

Internal metrics and participant-level data are omitted.

Opportunity areas

Three opportunity themes from the study

The opportunity areas focused on inbox organization, notification controls, multi-account use, and easier access across Outlook services and third-party clients.

01

Inbox organization

Help people clear accumulated junk, find critical messages, and make secondary and tertiary inboxes easier to use.

Based on accumulated secondary and tertiary inbox content
02

Notification controls

Provide contextual and selective notifications, with prioritization and focus controls.

Based on different account purposes and checking frequency
03

Access across endpoints

Support multiple accounts, easier return paths, and shortcuts between mobile web, Outlook apps, and third-party clients.

Based on mobile web's role as a fallback access point

What I learned

What I learned about retention research

Understanding retention required looking beyond the mobile website. Participants’ older accounts, devices, installed apps, and long-term email habits also affected how they used it.

The sequence of methods worked well. Existing evidence and stakeholder hypotheses informed the research questions, interviews provided detailed explanations, and the survey was then used to validate the qualitative findings.
