The brief

Supporting nonprofit leaders with limited staff time

New Sun Rising (NSR) helps Pittsburgh-area community organizations get started, find funding, and keep their programs running. Clients relied on its staff for grant advice and help using community data, but the small team could not provide continuous one-to-one support.

NSR's ask

Explore where AI could help staff support more nonprofit leaders.

I co-led research into NSR's services and nonprofit leaders' work. Interviews, fieldwork, and co-design led us to funding, then grant support: helping clients find opportunities, prepare applications, and report results.

  • 8 non-NSR leader interviews
  • Fieldwork with 5 NSR clients
  • 72 community survey responses

The grant process

What applying for a grant involves

Leaders first find a grant their organization qualifies for, then prepare an application explaining their program and funding needs. After receiving funding, they report what they delivered.

Finding a grant

Opportunities were spread across websites and word of mouth

Leaders searched funder websites and asked contacts about grants while also running their programs. They then had to check whether each opportunity fit their organization.

Grant recommendations

Bring opportunities together and help leaders shortlist ones that fit.

Preparing the application

Clients needed help using community statistics to make their case

NSR's census data and neighborhood indicators could help explain local needs. Staff spent time helping clients understand which figures were relevant. In our community survey, 51% found data hard to understand and 35% were unsure how to act on it.

Help with supporting data

Explain how a figure relates to the client's program and the grant they are applying for.

Reporting after funding

Clients had not always recorded activities and attendance

“well, I don't track that.”

A staff member recounted this reply when asking how many people a client had served. Missing records sometimes became apparent only when a grant report was due.

Collect missing details

Reuse existing records and ask about what is missing. Keep any reconstructed count labelled as an estimate.

“I really want to learn... learn how to do at least the basics in all stages.”

NSR client, discussing a pitch coach

The client wanted to learn how to make a convincing application. Another asked for supporting data and help reporting after receiving a grant. We focused on coaching clients through the task, while leaving the writing and final decisions with them.

Product & AI decisions

Vibrant Grants: from search to reporting

We designed a recommendation algorithm to help leaders shortlist grants, then AI assistance for choosing supporting data and collecting report details. I worked with the team on these decisions, considering operating cost and what NSR could maintain.

  • Criteria review: 5 NSR staff
  • 5 MHCI peer reviewers

Reporting prototype

Going deeper than an impact survey

NSR's impact surveys collected program information, but incomplete answers needed follow-up calls and interviews. A survey could not probe an answer; staff could, but those conversations took time and labor that a small team could not offer to every client.

We explored a chatbot that could ask follow-ups and help clients collect missing details for a grant report. In the prototype below, the assistant asks what activities were run, then combines existing survey records with information supplied in conversation. Clients approve the details before using them.

  • 9 prototypes · 18 participants across stakeholder groups
  • Think-aloud testing: 5 NSR clients + 2 other nonprofit leaders
  • Reporting walkthroughs: 4 NSR staff

AI evaluation

Evaluating the grant assistant

We turned AI-dependent tasks from four prototypes into 18 test scenarios. I defined what the assistant needed to do in each case, ran each scenario five times, and graded the responses against those expectations.

Scenarios covered

Records
Extract named fields, combine agreeing information, and identify gaps without inventing values.
Conflicts
Identify contradictory records and ask for clarification before continuing.
Sources
Attach the correct source and section to each claim.
Interpretation
Explain relevant community data and assess an individual grant against supplied criteria.
Writing
Use grant-appropriate language without adding specifics or overstating claims.
Conversation
Ask useful follow-ups, handle unavailable answers, respect requests to skip, and confirm before replacing work.

The model handled field extraction, citations, and several difficult client replies well. Failures helped us decide what to change in the system prompt and what to address in the UX.

Example of a failure

The reporting assistant needed a stopping point

In an additional conversation check, the assistant kept asking follow-ups after it had the required information. The client had no clear way to finish.

Observed: follow-ups continued

Required reporting information collected→More questions

Prototype change: client controls progress

Assistant signals enough information→Client chooses to move ahead

We added an enough-information message and a control for moving ahead in the prototype. Clients could finish collecting information without waiting for the assistant to stop probing.

Looking back

Reflection on the evaluation

Defining the expected behavior made grading more useful. I could look beyond whether an answer sounded right and ask whether it kept the sources intact, handled missing information honestly, and helped the client finish. The stopping failure showed why that last part mattered.
